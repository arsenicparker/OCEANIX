1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
======================================================================================
OCEANIX is a proposed student engineering project that uses real marine environmental data and machine learning to estimate fishing-zone suitability and present the result in an accessible map-based interface. Its purpose is to demonstrate a complete, defensible data-to-application workflow: acquire data, verify it, prepare features, train and evaluate a model, serve predictions through an API, and explain the output to a user.
The project is inspired by the general challenge of helping users interpret marine conditions, not by copying the full SIH ORCA system. OCEANIX will remain intentionally small: one verified prediction task, one reproducible ML pipeline, one API, and one responsive interface.

[TABLE]
Core constraint: The dataset audit comes before model development. A current list of PFZ points is useful for displaying advisory locations, but by itself it is not a complete supervised-learning dataset: it does not establish negative examples, matched environmental features, or actual fish abundance.
[/TABLE]

The project decision rule
-------------------------
  1. Use the Indian-coast route only if historical PFZ labels can be accessed in usable form and matched to suitable oceanographic features with enough data for an honest evaluation.
  2. If that route cannot be made defensible within a bounded effort, assess the NOAA/NCEI time-series. The NOAA fallback is a Western Indian Ocean skipjack-tuna task, not a Karnataka-specific PFZ model.
  3. If neither task is viable, narrow the project to a transparent environmental suitability analysis; label it as an index or exploratory tool, not a trained fish-catch predictor.

3. BACKGROUND AND MOTIVATION
======================================================================================
Finding productive fishing areas is challenging because marine conditions vary across space and time. Oceanographic measurements such as sea surface temperature (SST) and chlorophyll-a concentration can provide useful context for identifying conditions associated with potential fish aggregation. However, environmental variables are not fish counts: their relationship to fish distribution depends on species, season, local ecosystem, fishing effort and many other factors.
INCOIS describes Potential Fishing Zone (PFZ) advisories as a proxy for potential shoals of fish aggregation and states that its advisories use satellite-derived SST and chlorophyll information. The operational advisories include coordinates and contextual information such as depth, distance and direction from coastal reference points. PFZ zones can shift because ocean conditions are dynamic. [1]
OCEANIX applies a student-project lens to this space: collect a limited, well-understood dataset; select a prediction question the data can support; evaluate the model on unseen observations; and expose the output through a clear user interface. The value is not a claim that a student model is superior to official science services. It is an educational decision-support prototype that makes the data and its limits understandable.

Who could use a prototype?
--------------------------

[TABLE]
User / stakeholder | Need | How OCEANIX may help
------------------ | ---- | ------------------
Fisher or fishing-support worker | Understand where a model estimates favourable conditions | Map display, location/date, plain-language result and limits
Student / reviewer | See a complete ML engineering workflow | Reproducible data audit, metrics, source links and demo
Marine-data learner | Explore how environment and fishery labels relate | Transparent features, source provenance and simple explanations
Not an intended authority | Official advisories and safety decisions | OCEANIX must not replace official advisories, safety notices or local expertise
[/TABLE]


Guiding principles
------------------
  - Evidence before algorithms: inspect the downloaded data before designing a target.
  - Honest terminology: “suitability estimate” or “PFZ-like label” when that is all the labels support.
  - Reproducibility: fixed data versions, recorded preprocessing, saved model and tests.
  - Useful simplicity: one strong baseline and one justified model are better than many unvalidated models.
  - Clear limitations: display model date, study region, data coverage and uncertainty caveats.

4. PROBLEM STATEMENT AND OBJECTIVES
======================================================================================

Problem statement
-----------------
Marine environmental information and fishing-zone advisories may be distributed across sources and formats, while interpreting their spatial and temporal context can require technical knowledge. OCEANIX proposes to combine a verified set of environmental features with an appropriately defined historical target, train and evaluate a machine-learning model, and present its estimate through an understandable map-based interface. The system is intended to support interpretation and learning; it cannot guarantee fish presence, catch volume or safe sea conditions.

General objective
-----------------
To design and evaluate a practical ML-based marine intelligence prototype that uses real environmental and/or fishery data to estimate a clearly defined fishing-zone-related target and display the result with provenance and limitations.

Specific objectives
-------------------
  - Investigate official oceanographic and fishery data sources and document coverage, units, licensing and accessibility.
  - Define one target that is genuinely represented by the available labels or measurements.
  - Build a reproducible cleaning, alignment and feature-preparation pipeline.
  - Train a simple baseline and compare it with a suitable scikit-learn model when sample size and label quality justify the comparison.
  - Evaluate generalisation with a split strategy that considers time and geography, rather than reporting only training performance.
  - Expose the fitted pipeline through a validated API without retraining during each request.
  - Design a responsive web interface with accessible input controls and, when valid coordinates exist, an interactive map.
  - Provide documentation, tests, actual metrics, source citations, limitations and a reproducible demonstration.

Research questions
------------------
  - Can accessible historical data support a defensible prediction target in the chosen study region?
  - Which environmental variables are available and consistently aligned to the target?
  - Does a trained model outperform a simple baseline on unseen time periods or locations?
  - Can the output be communicated without implying certainty about actual fish presence or catch?

6. DATA STRATEGY AND SOURCE ASSESSMENT
======================================================================================
Dataset choice determines the honest scope of OCEANIX. The following is the recommended decision order, based on official source descriptions available when this blueprint was prepared. It is not proof that every historical file is directly downloadable or already aligned.

Route A - Indian coast using historical INCOIS PFZ labels
---------------------------------------------------------
INCOIS provides operational PFZ advisories and describes the use of SST and chlorophyll products to identify potential fish aggregation zones. Its data holdings page lists PFZ advisories from 2003 onward in ASCII/JPEG formats. This supports the relevance of investigating historical advisories, but access format and historical bulk retrieval must be tested before committing to training. [1][2]
  - Advantage: better alignment with an Indian coastal use case and the Karnataka advisory already reviewed.
  - Potential target: whether a location/time cell was included in an historical PFZ advisory; this represents an advisory label, not a direct observation that fish were absent or present.
  - Required join: PFZ advisory location/date plus environmental values for the same or justifiably matched spatial/temporal window.
  - Hard requirement: define defensible comparison points if classification is attempted. “Not listed in PFZ” must not automatically be called “no fish.”
  - Go/no-go test: obtain enough historical records in a machine-readable or reliably parseable form, match features, inspect coverage and document limitations.

Route B - NOAA/NCEI accession 0306675
-------------------------------------
NOAA/NCEI metadata describes the archive as “Weather, Ocean, and Tuna biomass time-series from 2005-2019 in the Western Indian Ocean.” It contains historical catch data obtained from IOTC and environmental variables sourced from Copernicus Marine Service. The metadata lists SST, sea-surface salinity, mixed-layer depth, sea-surface height, net primary productivity, chlorophyll-a, coordinates and month; two text files contain the compiled catch and environmental time series. [3][4]
  - Advantage: the archive is described as combining real historical fishery and environmental time-series.
  - Key limitation: the study area is the Western Indian Ocean and the target relates to skipjack tuna. It is not automatically a Karnataka-coast PFZ predictor.
  - Before choosing it: download and inspect the actual text files, determine the target field, unit of observation, sample count, missingness, spatial coverage and terms/attribution.
  - The model should be described specifically as a Western Indian Ocean skipjack-related task if this route is selected. Do not present its output as validated for Karnataka.

Environmental data route / access investigation
-----------------------------------------------
INCOIS maintains an ERDDAP server intended to make oceanographic datasets available in common formats, and its ocean-colour products page describes products such as chlorophyll, SST and related variables. Dataset catalogues, actual variables, date coverage, spatial resolution and download access must be checked dataset-by-dataset. [5][6]

Dataset decision matrix
-----------------------

[TABLE]
Criterion | INCOIS historical PFZ route | NOAA/NCEI 0306675 route
--------- | ------------------ | ------------------
Regional fit | Potentially strong for Indian coast; historical archive access must be confirmed | Western Indian Ocean; not India-coast-specific by default
Target meaning | Advisory presence/absence proxy if labels are responsibly constructed | Historical catch/biomass-related target; exact field and unit must be inspected
Environmental fields | SST / chlorophyll are relevant; exact match and historical coverage must be verified | Metadata lists several environmental variables; actual file schema still must be inspected
[/TABLE]

7. DATASET AUDIT AND EVIDENCE RULES
======================================================================================

What has already been reviewed
------------------------------
A Karnataka PFZ forecast spreadsheet was inspected during preparation of this blueprint. It states forecast validity from 4 October 2026 to 5 October 2026 and contains 24 non-empty PFZ location records. The table includes coastal reference name, direction, bearing, distance range, depth range, latitude (DMS) and longitude (DMS). Example landing/coastal reference names include Belekeri, Karwar, Honavar, Malpe and Mulki.

[TABLE]
Important interpretation: This file is useful for demonstrating parsing and mapping advisory points. It is a single short-validity operational advisory. It does not contain the environmental feature matrix or a verified negative-class dataset required to train a general supervised classifier by itself.
[/TABLE]


Mandatory dataset audit checklist
---------------------------------

[TABLE]
Check | Evidence required | Pass condition
----- | ----------------- | --------------
Identity and provenance | Official URL, dataset title, version/download date | Source and origin are documented
Time coverage | Earliest/latest timestamp; time zone/frequency | Enough history for meaningful split and evaluation
Geography | Coordinate bounds, coordinate system, spatial coverage | Matches declared project region and use case
Schema/units | Column names, field definitions, units and encodings | Every predictor and target has a documented meaning
Target validity | How target is measured or constructed | Target corresponds to the claim made in the UI/report
Feature match | Join key, time window, spatial matching method | Predictors align with the target without future information leakage
Sample quality | Row count, duplicates, missingness, impossible values | Quality is quantified and major issues are manageable
Class balance / target range | Class counts or target distribution | Evaluation strategy is appropriate; imbalance is handled honestly
Data rights | License, use and attribution notes | Use follows source terms and citation requirements
Repeatability | Saved raw source files/checksums or clear download instructions | A teammate can reproduce the dataset preparation
[/TABLE]


Stop/go rule
------------
  - GO: source is traceable; target is meaningful; there are enough observations for evaluation; time/location alignment is understood; split and metrics are defensible.
  - CONDITIONAL GO: data is usable only for exploratory visualisation or a clearly labelled environmental index. Reduce the claims and keep ML as future work if target labels are absent.
  - NO-GO: only a few positive points, no feature match, unknown units, unclear provenance, or a target manufactured from unverified assumptions. Do not train a classifier just to claim AI.

Data lineage record
-------------------
For every raw dataset, record: source URL, access date, filename, version if listed, date range, geographical bounds, file format, license/attribution, SHA-256 checksum if practical, number of rows, columns, preprocessing script and final output path. Never silently edit raw source files.

8. PREDICTION TASK, LABELS AND DATA SCHEMA
======================================================================================

Two possible target designs
---------------------------

[TABLE]
Route | Target candidate | What it can honestly claim
----- | ---------------- | ------------------
A: INCOIS PFZ | Advisory-class label for a defined location/time grid, only if historical PFZ and comparison points can be built defensibly | Estimate similarity to historical PFZ advisory labels in that dataset/region; not actual fish presence
B: NOAA/NCEI | The actual catch/biomass-related numeric target or a documented transformation after file inspection | Estimate that target for the represented Western Indian Ocean skipjack dataset; not Karnataka PFZ
Plan C: no valid label | A rule-based environmental score with explicit rules and provenance, if features support it | An exploratory index, not a trained ML prediction and not validated fish abundance
[/TABLE]


Proposed conceptual schema
--------------------------
The final schema must reflect the actual file. The following is a design template, not a claim that every column exists:

[TABLE]
Field | Type / example | Rule / purpose
----- | -------------- | --------------
observation_id | string or integer | Stable source row/record identifier where available
observation_time | date/time | Record time; preserve source timezone and resolution
latitude | decimal degrees | Convert DMS if needed; validate -90 to 90
longitude | decimal degrees | Convert DMS if needed; normalise to documented convention
sst | numeric, deg C | Only include when present, documented, and time/location aligned
chlorophyll_a | numeric, documented unit | Do not mix unit scales or different product definitions silently
other_environmental_features | dataset-specific | SSS, MLD, SSH, NPP etc. only if genuinely present and justified
target_value / target_label | numeric or class | Store exact target definition and source evidence
target_source | string | Advisory ID / catch source / record reference
data_split_group | date, region or cluster | Support grouped or chronological evaluation without leakage
[/TABLE]


Target design questions to resolve before coding
------------------------------------------------
  - Is the output classification or regression? The type of the actual target decides; do not preselect the answer.
  - What does one row represent: point, grid-cell/month, advisory location/day, or fishery reporting unit?
  - Can several target rows share the same environmental data because they are from the same date/grid cell? If yes, group them in evaluation.
  - If sampling non-PFZ comparison points, what is the sampling rule, and does it represent an unlabelled/no-advisory cell rather than fish absence?
  - Will the interface present the model as an advisory-pattern estimate or as catch/biomass prediction? That wording must match the target exactly.

9. DATA PROCESSING AND FEATURE ENGINEERING
======================================================================================

FIGURE - DATA-TO-MODEL WORKFLOW
1. Collect real source data: INCOIS PFZ and ocean variables, or validated catch/environment data.
2. Audit and align: verify schema, coordinates, dates, units, provenance and target labels.
3. Clean and engineer features: missingness checks, DMS-to-decimal coordinates, time/location joins.
4. Split data safely: hold out later time periods and/or locations to reduce leakage.
5. Train and evaluate: baseline first; Random Forest only if appropriate; report real metrics.
6. Serve a prediction: FastAPI validates inputs and loads the same fitted preprocessing pipeline.
7. Explain and display: responsive UI and map, with date, data provenance and limitations shown.


Processing stages
-----------------

[TABLE]
Stage | Implementation approach | Output / quality check
----- | ------------------ | ------------------
1. Raw ingestion | Read CSV/TSV/Excel/NetCDF only as needed; keep raw files immutable | Raw file inventory, data dictionary, source notes
2. Schema inspection | Print dtypes, null counts, min/max, unique values, date range and sample rows | Document unexpected fields/encodings before cleaning
3. Coordinate handling | Convert DMS to decimal degrees; validate hemisphere and range; retain original fields if needed | Check converted points on a map and compare sample records
4. Time handling | Parse dates using explicit format; preserve time zone and frequency; do not infer unsupported precision | Report malformed dates, duplicates and ordering
5. Feature alignment | Join by justified spatial/temporal rule; document grid, tolerance and time window | Join coverage and unmatched-row counts
6. Quality checks | Measure missingness, duplicates, impossible values, unit inconsistencies and outliers | No silent row dropping; report exclusion rules
7. Split first | Create grouped/temporal split before fitting data-dependent transformations | No information from final test set enters training transforms
8. Preprocessing pipeline | Use a reproducible scikit-learn Pipeline / ColumnTransformer when suitable | Same transformations at training and serving time
9. Export | Save model, pipeline config, schema and version metadata together | A clean environment can load and predict on a known test example
[/TABLE]


Specific guidance
-----------------
  - Do not impute a missing target. Remove or investigate rows without valid target evidence.
  - For numeric predictors, consider median imputation only if the missingness is understood; add missingness flags only if justified.
  - Scaling is usually unnecessary for tree-based Random Forests, but may be needed for models such as logistic regression or distance-based methods.
  - Do not fill unknown values with arbitrary zeroes; zero temperature/chlorophyll may have real meaning or be impossible depending on variable.
  - Record every filter, conversion and join in code. Save an audit log with row counts before and after each major step.

10. MACHINE-LEARNING PLAN
======================================================================================

Recommended modelling sequence
------------------------------

[TABLE]
Stage | Model / method | Reason
----- | -------------- | ------
Baseline | DummyClassifier / DummyRegressor or a simple domain-appropriate reference | Sets a minimum performance bar; catches models that do no better than trivial guesses
Primary candidate | RandomForestClassifier or RandomForestRegressor, chosen after target inspection | Can model non-linear feature interactions; robust starting point for tabular data
Optional comparison | Logistic/linear regression or HistGradientBoosting if data shape and time allow | Include only if it answers a real comparison question
Interpretability | Permutation importance and/or model-native feature importance; SHAP is optional | Gives a useful model-level explanation without adding unnecessary dependencies
[/TABLE]


Classification versus regression
--------------------------------
Classification is appropriate only if the target is a valid discrete class (for example, a documented PFZ-advisory label). Regression is appropriate only if the target is numeric (for example, a measured catch/biomass value with known units). Do not bin a continuous target into “low/medium/high” merely to simplify the UI unless the bins have a defensible purpose and are documented.

Training and validation protocol
--------------------------------
  - Create a frozen test set based on later time periods and/or held-out spatial groups when practical. Choose the split strategy from the sampling design, not convenience.
  - Avoid random row splitting when nearby coordinates, repeated observations, or same-date environmental grids can leak similar data across train and test.
  - Tune using only training/validation data. Evaluate the final locked model on the test set once after model decisions are complete.
  - Set random seeds for reproducibility where supported. Record Python and package versions.
  - Report sample counts by split and class/target distribution for every experiment.
  - Use cross-validation appropriate to group or time structure if enough independent groups exist; do not force k-fold CV on a tiny dataset.

Implementation sketch (conceptual)
----------------------------------
1) Load the audited feature table. 2) Define X using documented predictor columns and y using the audited target. 3) Build a preprocessing + estimator pipeline. 4) Split by time/location groups. 5) Fit the baseline. 6) Fit one justified model. 7) Calculate metrics and plots. 8) Save the complete pipeline and model metadata. 9) Run a reload-and-predict smoke test. This is a workflow outline; actual code and parameters must be based on the real data.

Feature leakage examples to avoid
---------------------------------
  - Using future dates or future environmental measurements to predict an earlier PFZ advisory.
  - Using coordinates that directly encode the advisory class because the target construction makes the location itself a label shortcut, without testing whether it generalises.
  - Joining target records to the nearest environmental row without limiting temporal distance.
  - Scaling, selecting features or imputing using the complete dataset before train/test separation.
  - Keeping duplicate or adjacent observations in both train and test when they are essentially the same event.

11. EVALUATION, INTERPRETABILITY AND LIMITATIONS
======================================================================================

Metrics
--------

[TABLE]
Task | Minimum reported metrics | Why
---- | ------------------ | ---
Classification | Confusion matrix, precision, recall, F1, balanced accuracy; PR-AUC if classes are imbalanced and probabilities are meaningful | Accuracy can hide poor minority-class performance
Regression | MAE, RMSE and R-squared, plus target distribution and an error plot | MAE is interpretable in target units; RMSE penalises large errors; R-squared alone is not enough
Spatial/temporal generalisation | Performance by held-out time period or geographic group where sample size permits | Shows whether the model works beyond near-duplicate training observations
[/TABLE]


Required result artefacts
-------------------------
  - A dataset summary: row counts, variables, missingness, region/date range and target definition.
  - A baseline-versus-model metric table using the same test split.
  - A confusion matrix or predicted-versus-actual/error plot appropriate to the target.
  - A brief statement of where model performance is weak, including groups or periods with limited data.
  - A saved test example showing expected input and actual output from the exported pipeline.
  - A clear “not a catch guarantee” notice in both the report and user interface.

Interpreting feature importance
-------------------------------
Feature importance indicates which input variables helped the fitted model on the evaluated dataset; it does not establish causation. Correlated predictors can divide or distort importance. Explanations should be phrased as model behaviour (for example, “SST contributed strongly to this model’s decisions”) rather than oceanographic proof that SST causes fish aggregation.

Limitations to state publicly
-----------------------------
  - Marine systems are dynamic, so an estimate based on historical data may not transfer to a different season, species, region or year.
  - PFZ advisory labels are a proxy for potential aggregation, not direct observation of fish presence, effort, catch, or economic outcome.
  - If the sample set is small or concentrated in a region/time, the model may not generalise beyond it.
  - Cloud cover, sensor coverage, missing environmental observations and differences across products can affect the available inputs.
  - The model does not incorporate all environmental, ecological, fishing-effort, regulatory or safety variables.
  - The prototype is not an operational navigation system or official advisory service.

12. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
======================================================================================

FIGURE - SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
Data sources (INCOIS / NOAA-NCEI) -> offline data pipeline (Pandas + validation) -> ML artifact (model + preprocessing) -> FastAPI service (input validation + output) -> responsive web UI (user input, result, limitations) -> interactive map (Leaflet + OpenStreetMap attribution). The map and UI are connected as part of the user-facing result. The diagram notes that scheduled ocean-data refresh, user accounts and a native Android app are optional future extensions, not MVP requirements.


Component responsibilities
--------------------------

[TABLE]
Component | Responsibility | MVP implementation
--------- | -------------- | ------------------
Raw data store | Preserve downloaded source files without modification | Local `data/raw/`; only files permitted for use
Preparation scripts | Parse, validate, align and document rows | Python scripts / notebooks; deterministic outputs
Model pipeline | Fit preprocessing and estimator; save artifact and metadata | scikit-learn Pipeline saved with joblib/pickle-style trusted local artifact handling
API layer | Validate requests, load artifact and return response | FastAPI; local development server
Frontend | Collect supported inputs, show result/provenance/limitations | Responsive HTML/CSS/JS or a lightweight chosen framework
Map | Plot only valid locations and labels, with attribution | Leaflet + suitable map tiles; OpenStreetMap attribution when its tiles are used
Testing/docs | Check predictable outputs and reproducible setup | pytest / unit tests / README / reports
[/TABLE]


Design principles
-----------------
  - Separate model training from inference. The API should load a fixed artifact and not retrain for each request.
  - Keep source data, processed data, model artifacts, documentation and UI code in separate folders.
  - The API returns the target type, units/class definition, data context, model version and limitation text where practical.
  - The user interface must not display example markers as real predictions. Demo fixtures are labelled “sample/demo only.”
  - Avoid cloud hosting until the local version works; local development avoids unnecessary hosting charges and credentials.

13. API AND APPLICATION BEHAVIOUR
======================================================================================

Minimal API contract
--------------------
The exact feature schema is defined after dataset audit. The examples below describe the contract shape, not final field names or numeric values.

[TABLE]
Endpoint | Method | Purpose | Example behaviour
-------- | ------ | ------- | -----------------
/health | GET | Confirm API and model are available | Returns status and model version; no user data required
/model-info | GET | Explain supported target, region, feature schema and limitations | Returns metadata from the saved model config
/predict | POST | Validate supported inputs and return estimate | Reject missing required features, impossible coordinates and unsupported schema
[/TABLE]


Request/response expectations
-----------------------------
Request should contain the verified input fields and, when necessary, a date/location identifier that allows features to be retrieved from the project’s local data store. Response should include the estimate, target name/type, probability or numeric value only if appropriate, model version, input summary, timestamp, and a brief limitation statement. Do not return a made-up confidence score if the model does not provide a calibrated probability.

API safeguards
--------------
  - Use request validation through typed schemas (e.g. Pydantic models) and meaningful 4xx errors for invalid requests.
  - Do not silently accept unknown feature names or arbitrary units.
  - Handle missing model artifacts and incompatible model versions with a clear service error.
  - Do not write user-submitted data or secrets to logs unless needed and approved.
  - Keep CORS restricted to the local frontend origin during development.
  - Use a single model loader and reuse the loaded pipeline; do not reload the model on every prediction unless required.

Example conceptual response
---------------------------
{
  "status": "ok",
  "target": "<verified target name>",
  "prediction": "<model output>",
  "model_version": "<version or commit>",
  "study_region": "<verified study region>",
  "limitations": "Historical-data estimate; not a guarantee of fish presence or catch."
}

14. USER INTERFACE AND MAP DESIGN
======================================================================================
The recommended MVP is a responsive web app that can be opened on a laptop or mobile browser. Figma can be used to design the screens first. A native Android implementation is optional future scope; it should not block validation of the core data and model.

Primary screens
---------------

[TABLE]
Screen | Elements | Behaviour
------ | -------- | ---------
1. Home / overview | OCEANIX name, short purpose, study region and data freshness | Explains what the system does and what it does not do
2. Prediction input | Date/location selector or supported feature fields; only the model’s real schema | Validates inputs; no fields are invented for visual effect
3. Prediction result | Class/suitability estimate or regression result; model/source/date context | Shows label/value and target meaning in plain language
4. Map | Valid coordinates, marker popup, legend, zoom, source attribution | Only maps genuine points; distinguish observed advisories from model predictions
5. About / data & limitations | Source links, methodology, coverage, model version and warnings | Builds trust and supports reviewer questions
[/TABLE]


Recommended map legend
----------------------
If historical advisories are shown as data points, label them “historical INCOIS advisory locations.” If model predictions are shown, label them “OCEANIX model estimates.” Never use the same marker or colour for both without a visible distinction. If the model output is a class, describe the class label and the training definition; if it is a numeric catch target, include units and the target’s spatial/time scale.

UX and accessibility checklist
------------------------------
  - Mobile-first layout with readable font sizes and high contrast.
  - Visible loading state when requesting predictions and readable error messages.
  - A “clear inputs” or reset action.
  - Do not require sign-in for the local MVP.
  - Display date, location, model version, and source context where relevant.
  - Provide a text alternative to the map result for users who cannot interpret colours or maps.
  - Show a disclaimer near the result, not hidden only in documentation.
  - Use OSM attribution if OpenStreetMap tiles are used and follow the selected tile provider’s usage policy. [7]

  4. Start FastAPI locally and check `/health` and `/model-info`.
  5. Start frontend and connect it to local API; test one valid and several invalid inputs.
  6. Run automated tests and follow README steps from a clean start to confirm reproducibility.

Definition of done for the MVP
------------------------------
  - The chosen data source and target are documented and defensible.
  - All preprocessing steps are scripted and repeatable.
  - Model metrics are measured on a held-out dataset and compared with a baseline.
  - No unverified real-world prediction is presented as actual catch or fish density.
  - API and UI can complete the main workflow locally.
  - The map distinguishes source observations/advisories from model estimates.
  - Critical test cases pass; known failures and limitations are documented.
  - Git history, README and project report reflect the state actually delivered.

18. RISKS, ETHICS AND RESPONSIBLE USE
======================================================================================

[TABLE]
Risk | Likelihood / impact | Mitigation
---- | ------------------ | ----------
Historical PFZ archive inaccessible or not machine-readable | High / High | Time-box research; inspect known sample; fallback to NOAA/NCEI; document decision
No defensible negative samples or target | High / High | Do not train binary classifier; redefine target or use a transparent non-ML index
Geographic mismatch | Medium / High | Declare the chosen region/species; do not claim Karnataka validity from WIO data
Environmental features do not align with target | Medium / High | Define matching windows and report unmatched rows before training
Data leakage inflates evaluation | Medium / High | Use temporal/spatial/group split, split overlap checks and frozen test set
Model has weak generalisation | Medium / Medium | Compare baseline; state limitations; revise scope instead of hiding metrics
Map looks authoritative despite uncertain output | Medium / High | Labels, confidence caveats, source/date and warning near every result
Live-data access breaks or costs money | Medium / Medium | MVP uses local historical data; live updates only as optional later scope
Agent consumes quota on repeated attempts | Medium / Medium | One bounded stage per prompt; stop on blocker; commit each milestone
Dependency / API incompatibility | Medium / Low | Minimal pinned requirements and smoke tests
[/TABLE]


Ethics and safety guardrails
----------------------------
  - OCEANIX is not official marine advice, a navigation tool, or a substitute for weather, cyclone, sea-state, fishing-ban or local safety communications.
  - A modelled “low suitability” value does not mean fish are absent; a “high” value does not guarantee catch.
  - Do not recommend sailing into dangerous conditions or imply the tool assesses marine safety.
  - Do not display sensitive personal information. The MVP does not need fisher accounts, personal location tracking or individual fishing histories.
  - Respect data-source licenses and attribution. Use official sources for provenance; do not redistribute data beyond allowed terms.
  - Make clear whether each map marker is a historical source point, an environmental observation or a model output.

Data and model governance
-------------------------
Record dataset version, source access date, feature schema, target definition, train/test split strategy, model parameters, software versions, evaluation metrics and known limitations. If the dataset changes or new rows are added, do not assume past metrics still apply. Re-run the audit and evaluation before describing the updated model as validated.

20. DEMO PLAN AND VIVA PREPARATION
======================================================================================

Recommended 4-minute demo flow
------------------------------
  1. Explain the problem and show the selected data source and study region.
  2. Show the dataset audit: what the target means, date coverage, key environmental fields and limitations.
  3. Show the evaluation results: baseline, final model and unseen-data metrics. Never invent numbers for the demo.
  4. Open the interface, select a supported location/date or input set, and generate one prediction from the API.
  5. Show the result on the map only if a valid coordinate exists; distinguish source advisory points from model estimates.
  6. Open the “About / Limitations” section and explain what the output can and cannot claim.

Likely viva questions and answer direction
------------------------------------------

[TABLE]
Question | Answer direction
-------- | ----------------
What problem does OCEANIX solve? | It helps interpret marine environmental data through a target-specific ML estimate and map-based interface; it is a prototype, not official advice.
Why did you choose this dataset? | Explain its official source, region, time coverage, variables and target; name verified limitations.
What does the model actually predict? | State the exact target and unit/class definition. Distinguish advisory labels from actual catch or fish abundance.
Why Random Forest? | A practical tabular baseline candidate that can learn nonlinear relationships; used only if it compares well to baseline and fits the data.
How do you prevent overfitting/leakage? | Use holdout by time/location/groups, fit preprocessing only on training data and report unseen-data metrics.
Which metrics did you use and why? | Classification: precision, recall, F1/balanced accuracy/PR-AUC as relevant. Regression: MAE/RMSE/R-squared and residual analysis.
Can high suitability guarantee fish or a catch? | No. The model predicts only its defined target and can be wrong; marine conditions and fishing outcomes are more complex.
Why not use deep learning? | Dataset size/format and the scope do not justify complexity; a simpler model is easier to validate and explain.
How is this different from the existing INCOIS PFZ service? | The project is an educational ML and data-visualisation prototype. It does not claim to replace or outperform the official operational service.
Can it predict for any Indian coastal point? | Only if the selected training data and environmental inputs support that area; no extrapolation claim without testing.
What if your model performs poorly? | Report it honestly, inspect data/label quality, compare with baseline and narrow the claim; weak results are useful findings.
What are your future improvements? | More validated historical data, independent/seasonal evaluation, user testing, responsible data refresh, multilingual UI and perhaps a native mobile app.
[/TABLE]
